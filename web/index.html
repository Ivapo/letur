Markdown on the left, a typeset page on the right, redrawn as you write.

Letur is an editor for writing that ends as a PDF. You write markdown — with captions, figures, references, math and citations — and beside it the page is set in the type you are reading now.

Open Letur

Runs in this browser. Nothing to install, and no server sees your text.

What a printed page has

A title block, notes at the foot of the column, a list of references: things a printed page carries and markdown has never had a way to ask for. Frontmatter · Footnotes · Citations

A leading --- block picks one of two looks, one or two columns, and whether display formulas are numbered. author takes several names separated by ;, and a ^ after one points at an affiliation. date is your text and nothing else — md2pdf never reads a clock.

What it refuses, and says so

Letur never quietly drops what it cannot set, because a PDF that leaves out part of its source looks finished and is wrong. It names the construct and the line instead, and these are the sentences it shows — checked against the compiler, like every example above.
